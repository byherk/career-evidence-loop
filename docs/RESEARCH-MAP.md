# Research Map

Career Evidence Loop sits at the intersection of several established research traditions.

The purpose of this document is to map those traditions, identify what they already contribute, and clarify where integration questions remain.

This is a living document.

## Intelligent Tutoring Systems

Relevant questions:

* How can instruction adapt to an individual learner?
* How can a system select an appropriate next activity?
* How can learning performance be monitored over time?

Potential contribution to this project:

Adaptive teaching and instructional sequencing.

## Learner Modeling

Relevant questions:

* How should a learner's changing state be represented?
* Which observations are informative?
* How should uncertainty be handled?

Potential contribution:

Longitudinal learner representations.

## Knowledge Tracing and Cognitive Diagnosis

Relevant questions:

* What knowledge or skills has a learner likely acquired?
* How does evidence from repeated performance update that estimate?

Potential contribution:

Methods for updating latent learner-state estimates.

Limitation for this project:

Many career-relevant capabilities are less structured than domains such as mathematics.

## Evidence-Centered Design

Relevant questions:

* What claim are we making about a learner?
* What evidence would support that claim?
* What task could produce that evidence?

Potential contribution:

A strong foundation for connecting claims, evidence, and assessment activity.

## Psychometrics

Relevant questions:

* Is an assessment measuring what it claims to measure?
* How reliable is the evidence?
* How should measurement error be represented?

Potential contribution:

Validity, reliability, calibration, and uncertainty.

## Authentic and Performance Assessment

Relevant questions:

* What can learners demonstrate through realistic tasks?
* Which capabilities are poorly represented by conventional tests?

Potential contribution:

Real-world evidence generation.

## Learning Analytics

Relevant questions:

* What can digital traces tell us about learner behavior?
* Which signals are useful, and which are easy to overinterpret?

Potential contribution:

Behavioral evidence and longitudinal interaction data.

## Educational Data Mining

Relevant questions:

* What patterns can be identified in learning data?
* How can those patterns support prediction or intervention?

Potential contribution:

Data-driven learner analysis.

## Dynamic Assessment

Relevant questions:

* How can assessment and learning intervention inform each other?
* What does responsiveness to support reveal about potential?

Potential contribution:

Learning potential rather than static performance alone.

## Human-in-the-Loop AI

Relevant questions:

* Which judgments should remain human?
* How should human and machine evidence interact?
* How can disagreement be surfaced rather than hidden?

Potential contribution:

Division of responsibility between AI and human experts.

## Vocational Psychology

Relevant areas may include:

* interests;
* self-efficacy;
* outcome expectations;
* goals;
* person-environment fit;
* career identity;
* environmental supports and barriers.

Potential contribution:

Theoretical models of career development and fit.

## Career Development

Relevant questions:

* How do people explore possible futures?
* How do career preferences evolve?
* How should uncertainty and changing identity be handled?

Potential contribution:

Career development as a process rather than a one-time matching problem.

## Occupational and Skills Taxonomies

Examples include occupational databases and competency frameworks.

Relevant questions:

* What skills, knowledge, abilities, activities, and contexts characterize different occupations?
* How can learner evidence connect to occupational requirements?

Potential contribution:

Structured representation of work.

## Recommender Systems

Relevant questions:

* How can alternatives be ranked?
* How can recommendations remain explainable?
* How can systems avoid narrow feedback loops?

Potential contribution:

Candidate career and learning recommendations.

## Meaningful Work and Decent Work

Relevant questions:

* What makes work sustainable, meaningful, or socially valuable?
* What does a person receive from work beyond income?
* How do structural constraints affect access to desirable work?

Potential contribution:

A broader concept of career fit.

## Emerging integration question

Many of these fields already address important parts of the problem.

The open question for this project is not whether those fields exist.

It is:

Can their insights be integrated into a longitudinal, evidence-seeking, human-in-the-loop system that supports career exploration without overstating what it knows?
