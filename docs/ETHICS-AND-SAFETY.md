# Ethics and Safety

Career Evidence Loop deals with potentially sensitive questions about ability, opportunity, identity, and future possibilities.

The project should therefore assume that incorrect conclusions can cause real harm.

## Learner agency

The learner should not become a passive object of prediction.

Recommendations should support reflection and exploration.

They should not become instructions about what a person must become.

## Privacy

Learner data may include:

* educational history;
* performance;
* interests;
* personal reflections;
* teacher comments;
* behavioral patterns;
* career aspirations.

Such information may be sensitive.

Public examples should use synthetic, fictional, or appropriately anonymized data.

## Children and young learners

Additional safeguards are required when working with minors.

Questions include:

* consent;
* parental or guardian involvement;
* developmental appropriateness;
* data retention;
* power relationships;
* long-term labeling effects.

This project does not yet define a complete policy for minors.

## Bias

Potential sources of bias include:

* unequal educational opportunity;
* cultural assumptions;
* socioeconomic status;
* disability;
* gender expectations;
* evaluator bias;
* historical labor-market inequality;
* biased occupational data;
* AI model bias.

A system must not interpret unequal opportunity as unequal human potential.

## Exploration bias

Recommendation itself changes what evidence becomes available.

If a learner is repeatedly given engineering experiences, the system may collect increasing evidence about engineering while learning very little about other possibilities.

This can create a self-reinforcing loop.

The system may therefore need explicit exploration mechanisms.

## High-stakes professions

For fields such as medicine, aviation, engineering, law, or other regulated and safety-critical professions, career exploration must remain distinct from professional qualification.

AI recommendation cannot replace:

* accredited training;
* licensing;
* professional supervision;
* formal assessment;
* safety requirements.

## Psychological harm

Career recommendations can affect:

* confidence;
* identity;
* motivation;
* perceived possibility.

Language should therefore avoid deterministic statements such as:

> "You are not suited for this."

Safer language may be:

> "We currently have limited evidence in this area."

## Explainability

A recommendation should ideally make clear:

* what evidence supports it;
* what evidence challenges it;
* what remains unknown;
* what assumptions were used;
* what could change the conclusion.

## Principle

> **The system should expand possibilities when evidence allows it, not prematurely close them.**
