# Career Hypothesis Model

## Purpose

The system should treat career directions as hypotheses that can become stronger, weaker, more specific, or less relevant over time.

A career hypothesis is not a prediction of destiny.

## Example

### Hypothesis

Professional cooking may be a promising direction.

### Supporting evidence

* sustained interest in practical food preparation;
* rapid improvement in kitchen skills;
* strong timing under pressure;
* positive feedback from an experienced chef.

### Contradictory or weaker evidence

* limited evidence of menu-development ability;
* unclear tolerance for long-term commercial kitchen conditions.

### Missing evidence

* performance during real service;
* teamwork across a full kitchen shift;
* long-term motivation.

### Next experiment

Complete a supervised service simulation and gather structured feedback.

## Proposed hypothesis components

A career hypothesis may eventually contain:

* hypothesis ID;
* career or career family;
* supporting evidence;
* contradictory evidence;
* missing evidence;
* contextual constraints;
* confidence;
* source frameworks;
* next experiment;
* learner reflection;
* review history.

## Updating hypotheses

A hypothesis may become:

* stronger;
* weaker;
* more specific;
* broader;
* disputed;
* inactive;
* replaced by another hypothesis.

The system should preserve why the change occurred.

## Important distinction

The following are not equivalent:

### Interest

"I enjoy this."

### Ability

"I currently perform this well."

### Learning potential

"I improve quickly when learning this."

### Career fit

"This kind of work may suit my abilities, motivation, context, and desired life."

The model should not collapse these concepts into a single score.

## Principle

Career fit is a hypothesis to test, not a verdict to accept.
