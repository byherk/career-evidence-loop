# Human in the Loop

## Why humans matter

An AI system has limited access to many forms of real-world performance.

It may analyze text, images, structured data, or digital artifacts, but some judgments depend on direct experience, embodied observation, professional context, or interpersonal interaction.

Examples include:

* tasting food;
* observing live performance;
* evaluating bedside manner;
* assessing field judgment;
* supervising safety-critical work;
* observing collaboration over time.

Human expertise is therefore not a temporary workaround.

It is part of the proposed architecture.

## Possible human roles

### Learner

Provides:

* goals;
* reflections;
* preferences;
* consent;
* interpretation of personal experience.

### Teacher

Provides:

* learning observations;
* assessment;
* developmental context;
* pedagogical judgment.

### Career counselor

Provides:

* career-development expertise;
* decision-support context;
* interpretation of constraints and opportunities.

### Domain expert

Provides:

* profession-specific performance judgment;
* authentic standards;
* realistic feedback;
* understanding of work conditions.

### Mentor or supervisor

Provides:

* longitudinal observation;
* professional behavior feedback;
* contextual interpretation.

## AI responsibilities

AI may assist with:

* organizing evidence;
* identifying patterns;
* tracking change;
* surfacing disagreement;
* finding missing evidence;
* proposing questions;
* generating possible experiments;
* connecting evidence with research and occupational knowledge.

## AI boundaries

AI should not:

* fabricate expert observations;
* treat all feedback as equally reliable;
* hide disagreement;
* infer professional qualification from weak signals;
* replace licensing or safety requirements;
* present uncertainty as certainty.

## Human disagreement

Experts may disagree.

The system should preserve:

* who made each judgment;
* the evidence they used;
* the criteria applied;
* the context;
* the disagreement itself.

Consensus should not be manufactured artificially.

## Principle

> **Human judgment should become traceable evidence, not invisible authority.**
