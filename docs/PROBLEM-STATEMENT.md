# Problem Statement

## Background

Learners make educational and career decisions under substantial uncertainty.

They may know what interests them today without knowing:

* what they could become good at;
* how quickly they could develop a skill;
* what kinds of work environments suit them;
* which abilities have never been tested;
* how their strengths translate into real occupations;
* what opportunities are realistically available;
* what forms of work would be sustainable or meaningful for them.

Traditional career guidance can provide valuable support, but access to high-quality counseling, mentoring, professional exposure, and authentic career experiences is uneven.

At the same time, AI systems increasingly interact with learners over long periods and may observe meaningful patterns in learning behavior.

This creates an opportunity, but also a risk.

The opportunity is to use AI to help organize evidence and support exploration.

The risk is that weak signals may be mistaken for strong conclusions.

## Core problem

A learner's digital behavior is not equivalent to a reliable model of their potential.

For example:

* repeated searches may indicate interest but not aptitude;
* high test performance may indicate current knowledge but not professional fit;
* low performance may reflect lack of opportunity rather than low potential;
* self-reported preferences may change with experience;
* AI may not be able to directly evaluate important real-world performance;
* expert feedback may itself be inconsistent or biased.

The central problem is therefore:

How can a human-AI system support longitudinal learning and career exploration while keeping its conclusions traceable to evidence, uncertainty, and real-world experience?

## Sub-problems

### Evidence

What should count as evidence?

How should evidence from different sources be represented and compared?

### Inference

How can the system distinguish observed behavior from interpretation?

### Uncertainty

How should the system represent what it does not know?

### Human expertise

When should teachers, counselors, mentors, or domain professionals provide judgments that AI cannot reliably make?

### Exploration

How can the system actively create opportunities to test uncertain career hypotheses?

### Bias

How can it avoid repeatedly recommending only the paths for which it already has evidence?

### Agency

How can learners remain active participants rather than passive subjects of prediction?

### Validity

How would we know that the system actually improves educational or career outcomes?

## Scope

This project currently focuses on conceptual and research questions.

It does not yet claim to provide:

* validated career predictions;
* professional qualification assessment;
* automated hiring decisions;
* psychological diagnosis;
* educational certification;
* safety-critical professional clearance.

The initial goal is to build a scientifically defensible way to ask better questions.
