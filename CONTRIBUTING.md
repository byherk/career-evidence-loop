# Contributing to Career Evidence Loop

Thank you for taking the time to contribute.

Career Evidence Loop is an early-stage, interdisciplinary research project. Contributions are welcome from researchers, educators, learners, career counselors, domain professionals, designers, engineers, and anyone who can help improve the underlying questions.

You do not need to agree with the current framework.

Criticism, counterexamples, competing models, and evidence that challenges the project's assumptions are welcome.

## What you can contribute

Useful contributions include:

* research papers and literature reviews;
* theoretical frameworks;
* examples from educational practice;
* alternative conceptual models;
* evidence-model proposals;
* ethical concerns;
* bias or fairness risks;
* failure cases;
* career-development perspectives;
* assessment methods;
* human-in-the-loop models;
* occupational frameworks;
* prototype ideas;
* data or schema proposals;
* clearer terminology;
* corrections to inaccurate claims;
* critiques of the architecture.

## Before contributing

Please keep the following principles in mind:

1. Separate observation from inference.
2. Do not present career hypotheses as deterministic conclusions.
3. Do not treat missing evidence as evidence of low potential.
4. Preserve learner agency.
5. Be explicit about uncertainty.
6. Prefer verifiable sources for empirical claims.
7. Do not include identifiable learner data without appropriate consent and governance.
8. High-stakes professional competence should not be inferred from AI interaction alone.

## Research contributions

When adding research, please provide enough information for another contributor to verify the source.

Where possible, include:

* author(s);
* year;
* title;
* publication or venue;
* DOI or stable URL;
* the specific idea relevant to this project;
* important limitations or cautions.

Avoid adding a paper only because its title appears relevant.

Please read enough of the source to explain what it actually supports.

## Literature notes

Working notes belong in:

`research/literature-notes/`

A literature note should distinguish between:

* what the source reports;
* your interpretation;
* how it may relate to this project;
* open questions;
* limitations.

## Proposing architectural changes

For substantial changes to the conceptual architecture, please explain:

* the problem you are trying to solve;
* the current assumption you are challenging;
* your proposed change;
* supporting evidence or rationale;
* possible risks;
* what would need to be tested.

An architecture proposal does not need to be complete to be useful.

## Examples

Examples should be:

* clearly fictional or appropriately anonymized;
* understandable without specialist knowledge;
* explicit about what is observed versus inferred;
* cautious about career recommendations;
* useful for testing the framework.

## AI-assisted contributions

AI tools may be used to assist with drafting, coding, summarization, or exploration.

Contributors remain responsible for:

* factual accuracy;
* source verification;
* appropriate attribution;
* avoiding fabricated citations;
* protecting private information;
* ensuring that the final contribution reflects their actual reasoning.

If AI materially shaped a research synthesis or analysis, consider noting that in the pull request.

## How to contribute

For small corrections:

1. Fork the repository.
2. Create a focused branch.
3. Make the change.
4. Open a pull request explaining what you changed and why.

For larger ideas:

1. Open an issue first.
2. Describe the problem or proposal.
3. Invite discussion.
4. Develop the contribution after the scope becomes clearer.

## Tone

Please aim for discussion that is:

* curious;
* evidence-aware;
* respectful;
* specific;
* open to revision.

This project is intended to become more accurate over time, not more certain for the sake of appearing confident.

Thank you for helping improve it.
