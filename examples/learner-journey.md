# Example Learner Journey

Fictional example for conceptual discussion.

## Initial signal

A learner repeatedly asks about cooking, watches food-preparation demonstrations, and voluntarily experiments with recipes at home.

Interpretation:

Possible sustained culinary interest

This is not yet evidence of professional cooking ability.

## Learning activity

The learner completes an introductory cooking course.

Observed:

* knife skills improve rapidly;
* timing improves across repeated tasks;
* hygiene is consistently strong;
* the learner remains engaged during practical work.

## Human feedback

An instructor reports:

* strong composure;
* good responsiveness to correction;
* above-average timing;
* inconsistent seasoning judgment.

## Initial hypothesis

Professional cooking may be worth further exploration.

## Missing evidence

The learner has not yet experienced:

* commercial service pressure;
* extended kitchen shifts;
* team coordination during service;
* repetitive production work.

## Next experiment

Participate in a supervised service simulation.

Collect structured feedback from an experienced culinary professional.

## Updated question

Not:

"Is this learner a chef?"

But:

"What did the next experience teach us about whether professional kitchen work remains a promising direction?"
