# Career Evidence Loop

> An open project exploring how AI, learners, educators, and domain experts can use evidence, real-world experiments, and continuous feedback to support learning and career discovery.

**Status:** Early concept / open research exploration

---

## The problem

Most career guidance systems try to answer questions such as:

- What am I interested in?
- What am I good at?
- Which careers might fit me?
- What should I learn next?

These questions are useful, but difficult to answer reliably.

A learner may frequently search for fashion, cooking, engineering, medicine, acting, or marine science. That shows **interest**, but interest alone does not prove ability, learning potential, or long-term career fit.

Likewise, an AI system may observe:

- questions;
- search behavior;
- learning interactions;
- written assignments;
- test results;
- digital projects.

But many important abilities cannot be reliably assessed by AI alone.

An AI cannot directly taste a learner's food like an experienced chef.

It may not judge a live theatrical performance like an acting coach.

It may not understand physical craftsmanship, field judgment, professional behavior, or real-world performance as well as an experienced human expert in that domain.

The challenge is therefore not simply:

> **How can AI recommend a career?**

The deeper question is:

> **How can AI help learners gather enough trustworthy evidence to discover which paths are worth exploring?**

---

## Core hypothesis

Career fit should be treated as a **hypothesis to test**, not a verdict to accept.

Instead of telling a learner:

> "You should become a designer."

a system could say:

> "Design is currently a promising direction based on the evidence we have. Here is why we think so, what evidence is still missing, and what experience could help us test that hypothesis."

The proposed loop is:

**Teach  
→ Observe  
→ Collect evidence  
→ Build a learner model  
→ Form career hypotheses  
→ Identify missing evidence  
→ Design the next experiment  
→ Receive human/expert evaluation  
→ Update the evidence  
→ Revise the learner model and career hypotheses**

The process repeats over time.

---

## Core principles

### 1. Interest is evidence of curiosity, not proof of aptitude

Searches, questions, reading behavior, and repeated curiosity are useful signals.

They should not automatically become claims about ability.

---

### 2. No recommendation should be stronger than the evidence supporting it

The system should distinguish between:

**Observed fact**

> The learner completed eight analytical tasks independently.

**Inference**

> This may indicate strong analytical reasoning.

**Career hypothesis**

> Analytical career families may be worth further exploration.

These are not the same thing.

---

### 3. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence of potential

A learner who has never tried acting, agriculture, industrial design, aviation, or marine science should not be classified as unsuitable for those fields.

The correct state may simply be:

> **Not yet assessed.**

---

### 4. Human experts are part of the system, not a workaround

Some evidence should come from people who can directly evaluate real-world performance.

Examples:

- chefs evaluating food and kitchen performance;
- designers evaluating portfolios and physical prototypes;
- acting coaches evaluating performances;
- teachers assessing learning behavior;
- engineers reviewing technical work;
- mentors assessing collaboration and professional judgment.

AI can then help organize, compare, interpret, and track that evidence over time.

---

### 5. AI should actively look for missing evidence

A career system should not only recommend based on existing data.

It should also ask:

> **What do we still need to learn about this person?**

If fashion design appears promising but material knowledge has never been tested, the system might propose:

> Create a small collection, select materials, build one prototype, and ask a professional designer to review it using a structured rubric.

The result becomes new evidence.

---

### 6. The learner remains the decision-maker

The purpose of the system is not to decide what someone must become.

It should help learners understand:

- what they appear to be becoming good at;
- what they enjoy;
- what environments they respond well to;
- what evidence supports different possible paths;
- what remains uncertain;
- what they could try next.

---

## A possible evidence model

Career hypotheses could draw from several evidence types.

| Evidence type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Interest | recurring questions, voluntary exploration, sustained curiosity |
| Demonstrated ability | assignments, projects, performances, technical work |
| Learning velocity | rate of improvement after instruction or feedback |
| Behavior | persistence, collaboration, response to ambiguity or pressure |
| Human assessment | teacher, mentor, coach, or domain-expert feedback |
| Artifacts | portfolio, code, writing, designs, performances, physical work |
| Real-world trials | internships, workshops, simulations, field experiences |
| Self-report | motivation, preferred environments, lifestyle goals |
| Outcomes | project results, certifications, customer/user feedback |

Different evidence types should have different levels of reliability and relevance.

## Example

Imagine a learner who shows strong interest in cooking.

Search history alone might support:

> **Possible culinary interest**

After several learning activities:

- knife skills improve quickly;
- timing under pressure is strong;
- hygiene is consistently excellent;
- the learner enjoys fast-paced kitchen work;
- a professional chef gives strong evaluations;
- creative menu development remains weaker.

The system could then produce:

### Career hypothesis: Professional cooking

**Supporting evidence**
- strong kitchen timing;
- rapid technical improvement;
- positive expert evaluation;
- high motivation during practical work.

**Contradictory or weak evidence**
- menu creativity is currently less developed.

**Missing evidence**
- performance during real service;
- long-term tolerance for kitchen working conditions.

**Next experiment**
- complete a supervised service shift and collect structured feedback.

The system does not conclude:

> "You are a chef."

It concludes:

> "Professional cooking is currently a well-supported direction worth testing further."

## Research foundations

Many parts of this idea already have established research traditions.

This project does **not** claim that these components are individually new.

Relevant fields include:

### Intelligent Tutoring Systems and Adaptive Learning
How learning systems adapt instruction to individual learners.

### Learner Modeling and Knowledge Tracing
How systems estimate what a learner knows and how that state changes over time.

### Evidence-Centered Design and Psychometrics
How claims about learner abilities should be connected to evidence and assessment tasks.

### Authentic and Performance Assessment
How real or realistic tasks can reveal capabilities that traditional tests may miss.

### Learning Analytics and Educational Data Mining
How learning behavior and digital interaction data can provide useful signals.

### Human-in-the-Loop AI
How AI and human judgment can complement each other rather than treating AI as an autonomous authority.

### Vocational Psychology and Career Development
Research into interests, self-efficacy, person-environment fit, career choice, and career development.

### Occupational and Skills Taxonomies
Systems such as occupational competency frameworks that describe the skills, activities, knowledge, and environments associated with different forms of work.

### Career and Job Recommender Systems
Systems that match people with possible occupations, jobs, skills, or learning opportunities.

### Dynamic and Adaptive Assessment
Assessment approaches that use interaction and new tasks to learn more about a person's developing ability.

## What may be different here

The possible contribution of this project is not a single new algorithm.

It is the integration of several existing ideas into a **longitudinal, evidence-seeking career-development loop**.

The proposed architecture treats career discovery as an ongoing process:

**Observation  
→ Evidence  
→ Competency hypothesis  
→ Career hypothesis  
→ Missing evidence  
→ Experiment  
→ Expert feedback  
→ Updated evidence**

Instead of asking only:

> "Which job matches this learner?"

the system asks:

> "What do we currently know?"

> "How strong is the evidence?"

> "What remains uncertain?"

> "What real experience should this learner try next?"

> "What changed after they tried it?"

## Contribution and return

Career fit may also involve more than ability and salary.

A longer-term version of this framework could explore two directions at the same time:

### What could the learner contribute?

What problems, communities, organizations, or areas of society could benefit from this person's developing capabilities?

### What could the learner receive in return?

For example:

- income;
- stability;
- autonomy;
- learning opportunities;
- meaningful work;
- social connection;
- recognition;
- flexibility;
- career mobility;
- lifestyle compatibility.

This suggests a broader relationship:

**Person ↔ Work ↔ Society**

rather than only:

**Person → Job**

## Open research questions

Many difficult questions remain unresolved.

### Evidence quality

How should evidence from different sources be weighted?

Is expert feedback more reliable than AI evaluation?

When should learner self-report matter?

---

### Expert disagreement

What happens when two qualified teachers or professionals disagree?

How should their judgments be calibrated?

---

### Cross-domain comparison

Can performance in cooking, engineering, acting, design, leadership, or scientific reasoning be meaningfully represented in one learner model?

Should they even be compared directly?

---

### Exploration bias

If a system initially believes a learner may be good at engineering, it may offer more engineering opportunities.

That produces more engineering evidence.

The system may then become increasingly confident simply because it explored that path more often.

How can the system continue discovering abilities that have not yet been tested?

---

### AI uncertainty

How should the system communicate:

- unknown;
- weak evidence;
- contradictory evidence;
- expert disagreement;
- changing ability?

A convincing explanation is not necessarily a correct explanation.

---

### Long-term validity

The most important question may require years to answer:

> Do learners who use this kind of system actually make better learning and career decisions?

---

### Fairness and access

Learners do not have equal access to:

- teachers;
- professional mentors;
- internships;
- equipment;
- travel;
- high-quality schools;
- career networks;
- safe opportunities to experiment.

A lack of opportunity must not be interpreted as a lack of potential.

---

## What this project is not

This project is **not** intended to build:

- an AI that decides a person's career;
- a deterministic personality-to-job matcher;
- a system that reduces people to a single score;
- a replacement for teachers, counselors, mentors, or domain experts;
- an automated hiring system;
- a system that treats current performance as permanent potential.

The system should support human development and decision-making, not replace them.

## Possible system architecture

A future implementation might contain several layers:

### Learning layer
Teaching, practice, exercises, projects, and adaptive learning.

### Evidence layer
Stores observations, artifacts, assessments, provenance, and confidence.

### Learner model
Represents developing skills, interests, behaviors, uncertainties, and evidence gaps.

### Career reasoning layer
Generates and updates career hypotheses.

### Experiment layer
Suggests experiences that could produce useful new evidence.

### Human expert layer
Allows teachers, mentors, and professionals to evaluate performance.

### Occupational knowledge layer
Connects learner evidence with skills, work environments, and career possibilities.

### Human decision layer
The learner, educators, counselors, and other trusted people interpret the evidence and make decisions.

---

## A possible long-term goal

The goal is not:

> **Tell me what I should become.**

The goal is closer to:

> **Help me discover what I could become, show me the evidence, help me test the possibilities, and keep updating the picture as I grow.**
